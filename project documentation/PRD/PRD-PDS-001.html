2. Executive Summary

PT. PPM develops new products whose packaging must pass through a long, multi-party chain before mass production can begin. Today that chain runs across paper forms, Word documents, spreadsheets and hallway handoffs. Nothing in the current state can answer, at any moment, the two questions management actually asks: which NPD products are stuck, at which packaging stage, waiting on whom, and what was the approved standard and who signed it.

This project delivers a Packaging Development System: a web application that models the NPD packaging process exactly as the authoritative flowchart defines it — concept and COGS target with BOD evaluation, marketing product brief and NPD timeline, packaging material sourcing, then the two parallel packaging tracks (primary, and secondary/tertiary) through technical drawing, supplier pitching and trial samples, T0/T1/T2 inspection, marketing design review, quotation, the packaging-material approval form, master product and standard creation, artwork and proofprint circulation, and finally scale up.

The system is built around one organising idea: a stage is never "done" until the party who owns it records a decision, and every decision is permanent, attributable and retrievable. That single property turns the flowchart from a picture into a working tracker, a document repository and an audit trail at the same time. Access is controlled per role per action using the supplied access matrix, and the two documents the business already signs — the packaging-material approval form and the packaging material specification — are generated by the system in their current official layout rather than re-typed.

Delivery follows a formal SDLC: this PRD is approved first, then the FSD, then the TSD, then phased development. Scope is deliberately bounded to the NPD flowchart plus the supplier portal and the QC module, with explicit exclusions in §5.2.

3. Background and Problem Statement

3.1 Current process

Packaging development for a new product involves at least eight internal parties (Marketing, BOD, R&D/Packaging Development, SCM, QC, QA, and the supplier) and three packaging categories that do not progress at the same speed. A primary pack such as a crimp pump requires mould/dummy approval before tooling is committed; a secondary pack such as a sticker requires artwork and colour-range circulation. These run in parallel and rejoin only near the end, at scale up.

3.2 Problems observed

#ProblemConsequence today
1No single view of process stateManagement cannot see which stage an NPD packaging item is at or who is holding it; status is gathered by asking people.
2Decisions are not attributableApprovals on paper or in chat cannot be reliably reconstructed later; when a mass-production defect traces back to packaging, the approved standard and its sign-off cannot be produced quickly.
3Parallel branches desynchronisePrimary and secondary/tertiary tracks advance independently; one branch reaches quotation while the other is still at drawing review, and nobody is forced to reconcile before scale up.
4Documents are re-typedThe packaging material specification and the approval form are recreated per product in Word, introducing transcription errors in test parameters, tolerances and effective dates.
5Supplier interaction is unrecordedPitching, blank samples, quotations and supply-service evaluations live in email; there is no comparable history to judge a supplier on, and no trail when a quotation changes.
6Inspection results are not structuredT0/T1/T2 technical and visual results are captured as free text, so pass/fail trends per packaging item cannot be measured.

5. Scope

5.1 In scope

  1. The complete NPD packaging process exactly as the authoritative flowchart defines it: concept development with COGS target and BOD evaluation; product summary and NPD form by Marketing; NPD timeline; packaging material sourcing; the primary packaging branch; the secondary and tertiary packaging branch; and scale up as the exit of both branches.
  2. Master data: packaging items and categories (primary/secondary/tertiary), suppliers, packaging material specifications.
  3. Supplier portal — a restricted supplier-facing area for pitching and blank-sample submission, quotation entry, and receipt of artwork/proofprint requests and revision notes.
  4. QC module — T0/T1/T2 inspection with per-parameter pass/fail, application and transport testing, and colour-range circulation.
  5. Approval workflows with permanent decision history, in-app and email notifications, audit trail, dashboards, and PDF/Excel export of project, inspection, supplier-evaluation and approval records.
  6. Generation of the two official documents — Form Approval Bahan Kemas and Spesifikasi Bahan Pengemas — in their existing layout.
  7. Administration: user management, role assignment, configurable access matrix, announcements, password reset.

5.2 Out of scope

  1. The substitution-of-packaging-material process as specified in the rejected Excel sheets. The flowchart does not contain it. If the business still needs substitution, it is a separate change request and a separate PRD — see OQ-02.
  2. EPD (existing product development) as a distinct process. The flowchart models NPD only. The data model will not prevent adding EPD later as a project type.
  3. Integration with ERP, inventory, procurement or finance systems; no purchase orders, stock or costing postings leave this system.
  4. Legally certified digital signatures. Approval is recorded as an attributed in-system decision plus a printed signature block, matching how the paper form works today.
  5. Native mobile applications; the web interface is responsive but no app is delivered.
  6. Formula/product development, stability testing, and regulatory/registration workflows.
  7. Migration of historical paper records. Legacy data is loaded only where it exists in usable electronic form (see §17 dependencies).
  8. Multi-language interface. The interface is delivered in one language; see OQ-03.

7.3 Branching and synchronisation rules

IDRuleStatement
BS-01Branch selection at sourcingAt NPD-04 each packaging item is assigned to exactly one branch by its category: primary → Branch A; secondary and tertiary → Branch B.
BS-02Branches run independentlyNo stage in Branch A waits for a stage in Branch B, and vice versa. Progress is tracked per branch.
BS-03Different internal orderBranch A reviews and approves the drawing before supplier evaluation and dummy review. Branch B creates the drawing, then pitches suppliers, then inspects, then has Marketing review the design. The system must not force one order on both.
BS-04Scale Up is per branchEach branch reaches its own Scale Up. A project is fully complete only when every branch that has at least one packaging item has closed.
BS-05Project-level visibilityProject view shows both branches side by side with the current stage of each, so desynchronisation is visible rather than discovered.
BS-06Empty branch is not a blockerA branch with no packaging item is marked not-applicable and never blocks project completion.

7.4 Status model

Two orthogonal statuses apply to every stage record: the document status (where the record is in its life) and the approval status (where it is in the decision chain). Separating them is what makes "stuck, waiting on whom" answerable.

Document statusApproval statusMeaning
Draft—Being prepared by its author; not yet visible to approvers.
SubmittedSubmittedHanded to the checker; author can no longer edit it.
PendingPending checking / Checking / CheckedWith the checker (QC Supervisor) before it reaches approvers.
PendingWaiting approvalChecked and released to the approver(s); all approvers must approve.
ClosedApprovedAll approvers approved. The stage is complete and the record is locked.
CancelledRejectedOne or more approvers rejected. Returns to the author with a mandatory reason; a new version can be raised.
Derived, not asserted. Approval status is always computed from the recorded decisions of the assigned approvers, never set directly by a user. This prevents a record from reading "Approved" while an approver's decision is missing.

7.5 Rejection and rework loops

  1. Any rejection at any stage returns the record to its author with a mandatory reason; the reason is retained permanently against the record.
  2. Re-submission creates a new version of the record. Prior versions remain readable; nothing is overwritten.
  3. A rejected concept at NPD-01 returns the project to Marketing and does not create downstream branch records.
  4. A failed inspection at P-04 / S-03 opens a rework task to the responsible party and re-enters the same trial level; advancing to the next trial level requires the previous level to have passed.
  5. Rejected artwork at S-07 returns to the supplier through the portal with the commenting party's note attached.
  6. Scale Up cannot be recorded while any earlier stage of the same branch is open.

9. Business Rules

IDRuleStatement
BR-01Author cannot approve own documentThe person who authored a record is never an approver of that same record version.
BR-02All approvers must approveA document becomes Approved only when every approver assigned to it has approved; a single rejection makes it Rejected.
BR-03Rejection always carries a reasonA rejection cannot be recorded without a reason; the reason is permanent.
BR-04Approved records are immutableAn Approved record and its decisions cannot be edited. Change requires a new version.
BR-05Sequential trial levelsAll three trial rounds are mandatory for every packaging item in both branches: T1 requires T0 passed, T2 requires T1 passed, and the inspection stage closes only after T2 passes (FR-1105, OQ-05).

BR-10Supplier data isolationA supplier sees only its own quotations, samples, artwork and evaluations (AC-06).

13. Generated Documents

13.1 Form Approval Bahan Kemas

Generated from stored data in the official layout: document identity; packaging and project identification; the sample verdict checkboxes ("sample acceptable" / "sample not acceptable") and notes; and three signature blocks — prepared by, checked by, approved by (Quality Control Manager, SCM Manager, QA Manager) — each populated from real in-system decisions with name, role and date. The template convention "strike out what does not apply" is reproduced on the printed form (FR-1603).

13.2 Spesifikasi Bahan Pengemas (SBK)

Generated in the official layout with the organisation header, issuing departments, document number and effective date, the prepared / checked / approved blocks with names and dates, packaging material name, material type, and the parameter table grouped as physical examination, dimensions, weight, leak test, strength test, and design/marking conformity — each row carrying specification, measured value and method of analysis (FR-1703, FR-1704).

Layout fidelity is an acceptance criterion. Both documents must be reviewed against the current paper originals before UAT sign-off; a generated document that a department would not sign is a defect, not a preference.

17. Assumptions, Constraints, Dependencies, Risks

17.1 Assumptions

  1. The flowchart represents the process as it should run, not merely as it once ran.
  2. One user holds one primary role for workflow purposes; multi-role users resolve to the union of their grants.
  3. Both branches of a project concern the same product but different packaging items, so they can close independently.
  4. Suppliers can be given accounts and are willing to submit samples and quotations through the portal rather than by email.
  5. Existing specification data is available in a usable electronic form for import.

17.2 Constraints

  1. Technology stack is fixed by the stakeholder: PHP Laravel with React front end and MySQL database.
  2. Runs on the organisation's existing local development and hosting environment (Laragon/Windows toolchain available on the delivery machine).
  3. Documents must remain printable on the existing A4 stationery and be signable by hand.
  4. No budget assumed for third-party signature, storage or integration services.

17.3 Dependencies

  1. Confirmation of the five under-specified roles (§6.1 gap) before the FSD fixes permissions.
  2. Provision of current paper originals of both generated documents for layout comparison.
  3. Mail delivery configuration for notifications and password reset.
  4. A named product owner with authority to arbitrate flowchart-versus-Excel conflicts.
